What Is Komodo Island

Komodo Island is the island that gave the national park, and the world's largest lizard, its name. Its dry hills, savanna and mangrove-lined bays are home to wild Komodo dragons, along with deer, wild boar and many bird species.

Where Is Komodo Island

Komodo Island lies in the west of Komodo National Park, East Nusa Tenggara, between Sumbawa and Flores. Boats leave from Labuan Bajo on Flores. The crossing takes around 3 to 4 hours by phinisi, or about 1.5 to 2 hours by speedboat, depending on the sea.

Why Komodo Island Is a Must-Visit

It is one of the very few places on earth where you can see Komodo dragons in their natural habitat. Walking the island with a ranger, close to animals that have lived here for millions of years, is the moment many guests remember long after the trip.

What to Expect in Komodo Island

Visits start at the Loh Liang ranger station, and a park ranger leads every walk. Trails of different lengths pass through dry forest and open savanna. Expect heat, little shade and dusty paths. The dragons are wild, so sightings are likely but never guaranteed.

Best Time to Visit Komodo Island

The dry season, from about April to November, brings calmer seas and easier treks. April to June is a lovely time, when the hills are still green. Mornings are cooler and the dragons are often more active. From December to March, rain and stronger waves can change boat routes.

Things to Do in Komodo Island

  • Join a ranger-guided trek to see Komodo dragons in the wild
  • Hike to a hilltop viewpoint over the bay
  • Visit Kampung Komodo, the island's fishing village
  • Look out for deer, wild boar and the yellow-crested cockatoo
  • Combine your visit with nearby Pink Beach
